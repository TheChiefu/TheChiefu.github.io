Work, School, and Life

Written: 2025-09-05

Doing my Master's Degree

Options, options, options

Before I even started the job I have now I was looking at different paths I wanted to take when I got laid off 2-3 years ago. When I moved back home I was thinking on if I still wanted to move to Japan, if I wanted to go to language school there, if I wanted to find a job in RI, if I could find a job in a new state, continue edutation, etc. I felt liked I was tugged in so many different directions that I wanted to go towards. A few months after sitting on it I kept thinking back on the last months of college.

Back then I was still job hunting and felt defeated by the fact no one was reaching back out or responding to any applications I sent. I started to think that I'd have to graduate and be unemployed for a while until I found a job. I also kept on thinking about post-graduate eduation and doing a master's degree. I looked at the URI website and perusing the Computer Science degree looked exciting. However by the time I was actually starting to consider it I got an email from GM about a potential job opportunity. So, the idea of continuing education eluded my for a while. I didn't think about it much for a few years, but when I moved back it came up again.

I read about different programs online during my bordem and came accross Georgia Tech. I heard a lot of good things and I started reading posts about people who went through the program; it seemed tough but fair. But more importantly it looked like it could really teach me more about concepts I felt like I didn't get to touch on much in undergrad. After some time bugging my old managers and teachers from school, I got 3 letters of recommendations, wrote my essay, and sent off my application. For whatever reason I didn't have much expectation of getting in and was not expecting to hear anything back (other than a rejection).

Months go by, I start my new job and I get an email from Georgia Tech. I ammused (thinking I got a rejection email as I anticipated), opened it up. To my suprise it was an acceptance letter with instructions on how to get started before my first semester. I was honestly shocked, I didn't realize until later that the acceptance rate was high; but was very happy. I got the email in Fall of 2024 and started my degree in Spring of 2025.

I excitedly looked at the class list for my first semester and picked "Video Game Design" and "Game AI"; ready to start my journey.

Starting Up School Again

2024 went by and I started the second week on 2025 getting the hang of using Canvas again (probably since high school), setting up all my bookmarks, IDE configurations, and installing the required programs. I navigated over to the specialization tab on BuzzPort and selected Computer Graphics, so that I know what classes I needed to take for my degree.

I lucked out that the first classes I picked in the program used a program I was already familiar with (Unity), but I guess it was a good introduction to the program.

One thing I kept thinking about though was how to manage myself with all these new responsiblities. At the start of the year I was working full-time, doing a master's degree part-time, and learning Japanese. Something had to go, and I felt like I knew what was coming.

As much as I wanted to keep up my streak of studying everyday for 4 years, I decided that I'd cut out all Japanese studies. On December 31st of 2024 I did my last Anki deck for a long time and haven't opened it since. Some part of me is sad that I cut this part out of my life, but another part is sighing a breath of relief. I written about it before, but my studying has been a huge source of stress and I feel like taking a 2-3 year break will really do me good.

It's not like I can't interact with anything Japanese, it's just that I won't be mulling over flash cards decks or grammar exams anymore. Now I could spend my full attention on school and work.

Video Game Design

The Video Game Design class for me was mostly refresher content since I spent most of my life entrenched in game development videos, behind the scenes content, and so on. But I did learn new things about game design I didn't before such as: root-motion based movement for animation/character controlling and how to specifically use Inverse Kinematic systems (in Unity). The one thing I was a bit nervous about was working on a group project, since it was required.

In the past I've had okay to bad experiences working with groups in school. Most of the time I mostly do the work myself and submit for a group to get it out of the way. More often than not most group assignment in school were forced and nobody wanted to be there in the first place. However, this time was different because it's a masters program and a class people specifically choose to be in (it's an elective). I felt like I lucked out, our group got straight to forming a game plan and met up at least once a week.

I felt a little like a teacher since I was so used to Unity/C# already and it was new to everyone else, but there all were so interested in learning it that I was happy to help. One of my classmates especially was fun to work with and talk to. I won't say his full name but TJ was so into learning about game dev we had hour long conversations about the topic (even to the detrement of my sleep sometimes).

Back to the class at hand, the majority of it was time spent making a group project game and our team decided on a parkour game. Some people worked on level design, others AI, and I worked on the main character. Well, not the model itself (none of us are 3D artists). We used free models/animations on Maximo and I spent copious amounts of hours learning how to use the animation state machine, IK, and getting the feel of movement just right.

I kind of felt like I was cheating a little bit since I did some of this stuff already when working on Taurus, but I purposely spent time on learning how to make a better character in class so it could benefit me in my personal projects. The part I felt most proud of was figuring out "grounded" mechanics (determining if you are in the air or not) using raycasts, sliding mechanics (changing the hitbox in realtime), and adding climbing (root-based animation / mix of toggling hit boxes). It felt like I was actually making something that would appear in a real production game.

Game AI

At the same time as my other class I was also taking Game AI. Again I lucked out in that it uses Unity and I had the same professor as the first class, however unlike the first class this one went more in-depth with the topics that I not too familiar with.

The first class was more of watching a lecture, implement what you just watched or read, and repeat. This class was more, watch the lecture, read about the topic in depth, and spent hours or days trying to get a solution you think is good enough for the 2 auto-graded submissions you have. So a lot higher steaks and more "in the weeds" coding.

I thought it was very useful information though, even if the class almost pushed me over my limits sometimes (I had a few times were I felt like dropping the class). The topics we covered were:

  • Discretized grid creation of a game world
  • Path/Node Network navigation
  • Nav Mesh creation (where nodes/portals are, nav-mesh agent radius, mesh boundaries)
  • A* Search Implementation
  • Throwing Prediction (tracking target and predict a throw to hit the target, in 3D with gravity)
  • State/Behavior Machines for a Dodgeball AI
  • Fuzzy Logic for driving a car on a racetrack
  • Procedural Map Generation with Biomes

The first couple of projects were challenging but I eventually put a few weekends into them and solved the assignments with near 100s. However towards the middle is where it got tough and I almost dropped the class since it felt so overwhelming. But I stuck it out and eventually solved the problems, with some help from the recommended text book; going over the chapter several times.

After I finished a good amount of my projects I had so many ideas of game projects and systems I wanted to build into a game. I thought about noids and group behavior logic discussed in the class lectures, how cool it'd be to have a Crysis-like game but on an large open map where you take out tiers of enemies (similar to Crackdown); but the game AI is smart and has complex behavior.

For example, a group of 4 enemies are on patrol. The AI hears the player in a bush and instructs 2 of the others to check it out; one stays still on the path and the last one looks around where the rest aren't. Then if the player is spotted they can try to flush them out. One might throw a grenade near or at the player location (maybe intercepting), the others try to flank on either side, and take them out. So on and so forth, I was brimming with new ideas; especially now that I had the book and could re-review sections I thought were interesting.

Towards the end of the semester I was starting to feel burn out and at times felt like my body was too tired to do anything. Keeping up with this class and the group video game project was a lot on me. It wasn't until around April until I realized what was really happening though.
